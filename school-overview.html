Supporting learning with interactive tools

Over the past year I have built a collection of free, browser-based tools, activities and projects to support learning: in Computer Science and Maths first, and increasingly across other subjects. Everything here runs on a phone, Chromebook or school laptop with nothing to install, no login and no personal data stored. This page groups the work by what it does for students and staff, with a link to each live tool.

How it is built

Free, for any department

Everything is published openly. No licence, no per-seat cost, no renewal.

Browser only

Nothing to install; runs on Chromebooks, Windows laptops and phones, and keeps working when the network blocks external libraries.

No logins, no personal data

Students are never asked to sign up. Progress stays on the device; where classes are tracked, anonymous class codes are used.

Offline where it matters

The revision games work offline once opened, and the captions tool runs with no internet at all, in line with the school's AI policy.

Inclusion by default

Low reading age, read-aloud, calm mode and one-step-at-a-time are built in, not bolted on.

Built in the classroom

Each tool exists because a lesson needed it; the ones that did not help were dropped.

What this could do for the school next

Ready now, needs a nudge

  1. Whole-school key terms. Each department supplies one term list per unit; the revision games then cover every subject by half-term.
  2. Diagnostic questions in maths. Use the projector mode as the default starter in KS3 maths; no devices needed.
  3. Parents' site. Extend the weekly-topics page beyond Maths and English, and add languages the school needs.
  4. Route to a 4 for intervention groups. One link per student, pre-set to their support settings, for the engagement trial and Year 11 intervention.

Needs a decision

  1. Live captions pilot. Agree a deployment IT support is comfortable with (a server on the school network with internet blocked, or a per-classroom laptop) and trial it with two or three deaf and EAL students.
  2. KS4 shared-concept map. Ten minutes at a heads-of-department meeting for each department to confirm its sequencing.
  3. Coding club capacity. A second lunchtime or a KS4 session if demand holds.
  4. Island resource bank. As Jersey moves to shared resources across schools, this collection is ready to be offered as a starting contribution.
