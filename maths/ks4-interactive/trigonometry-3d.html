Pythagoras and Trigonometry in 3D Higher

How long a pole fits in a box? How steep is the edge of a pyramid? In three dimensions, the right-angled triangles you need are still there, but they're tilted, so they look wrong in a flat drawing. The method is always the same: find the right-angled triangle, pull it out and draw it flat, then use Pythagoras or SOH CAH TOA as usual.

GCSE subject content G20 (Higher): apply Pythagoras' theorem and the trigonometric ratios to find angles and lengths in right-angled triangles in three-dimensional figures.

1 · Right angles that don't look like right angles Higher

You'll need Pythagoras (KS3 Pythagoras) and SOH CAH TOA (Trigonometry and exact values). A cuboid is a box shape: six rectangular faces, every corner a right angle. Its corners are usually labelled ABCD round the bottom and EFGH round the top, with E above A, F above B, and so on.

Drag the box to turn it. Each button lights up a right-angled triangle hidden in the cuboid. In a slanted drawing the 90° corner rarely looks like 90°, but it is one, and that's what matters.

Drag to rotate. Hidden edges are dashed.

2 · The space diagonal in two steps Higher

The space diagonal AG goes from one corner, through the inside of the box, to the opposite corner. It's the longest straight line that fits in the box. No face contains it, so you need two right-angled triangles, one after the other:

  1. Across the base: AC is the hypotenuse of triangle ABC, so AC² = a² + b².
  2. Up through the box: AG is the hypotenuse of triangle ACG, which stands upright with the right angle at C (CG is vertical, AC lies flat on the floor). So AG² = AC² + c².

Put the two steps together: AG² = AC² + c² = a² + b² + c². So you can do it in one go:

Space diagonal of a cuboid d = √(a² + b² + c²)

Common mistakes: finding only the base diagonal AC and stopping; forgetting the final square root; and, if you work in two steps, rounding AC before using it. Keep AC as a surd (√25) or the full calculator value.

3 · The angle between a line and a plane Higher

A plane is a flat surface that goes on for ever, like the floor or a face of the box extended. What is "the angle between the line AG and the floor"? Imagine a light directly above shining down: the line casts a shadow on the floor (its projection). The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its shadow.

To find it: from the top end of the line, drop a perpendicular to the plane (straight down onto the floor). That makes a right-angled triangle: the line is the hypotenuse, the shadow is the adjacent side, and the perpendicular is the opposite side. Choose a plane below and watch the triangle change.

Exam trap: the adjacent side must be the shadow (from the foot of the line to the foot of the perpendicular), lying in the plane. Using an edge such as AB instead of the diagonal AC gives the wrong angle. For AG and the base of an 8 × 6 × 5 box, tan θ = 5 ÷ 10 (θ = 26.6°), not 5 ÷ 8 (32.0°).

4 · Pyramids Higher

In a right pyramid the apex (the top point) is directly above the centre of the base. The vertical height goes from the apex straight down to the centre, M. Three triangles come up again and again:

  • Slant edge (apex to a corner): vertical height and half the base diagonal.
  • Slant height of a face (apex to the middle of a base edge): vertical height and half the base width.
  • The angle between a slant edge and the base: tan θ = height ÷ half-diagonal.

5 · Wedges (triangular prisms) Higher

A wedge is a ramp: a rectangular base ABCD, a vertical back face DCFE, and a sloping face ABFE. Questions ask for the diagonal of the slope from A to F, or the angle that line makes with the base. Same method: it's the space diagonal of the box the wedge fits in, and the angle uses the base diagonal AC.

Worked examples

A cuboid measures 3 cm by 4 cm by 12 cm. Find the length of its space diagonal. Higher
Step 1, base diagonal: AC² = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, so AC = 5 cm.
Step 2, upright triangle ACG (right angle at C): AG² = AC² + CG² = 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169.
AG = √169 = 13 cm. One go: √(3² + 4² + 12²) = √169 = 13 ✓.
A cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB = 8 cm, BC = 6 cm and CG = 5 cm. Find the angle between AG and the base ABCD. Higher
Shadow of AG on the base: G is directly above C, so the shadow is AC, and the triangle is ACG with the right angle at C.
AC = √(8² + 6²) = √100 = 10 cm.
From angle θ at A: CG = 5 is opposite, AC = 10 is adjacent → tan θ = 5 ÷ 10 = 0.5.
θ = tan⁻¹(0.5) = 26.565…° = 26.6° (1 d.p.). (Using AB = 8 instead of AC would give 32.0°: wrong.)
A right pyramid has a square base of side 6 cm and vertical height 10 cm. Find the length of a slant edge and its angle with the base. Higher
Half the base diagonal: diagonal = √(6² + 6²) = √72 = 6√2, so half is 3√2 = 4.242… cm (the distance from the centre M to a corner).
Slant edge (triangle apex–M–corner, right angle at M): edge² = (3√2)² + 10² = 18 + 100 = 118, edge = √118 = 10.862… = 10.9 cm.
Angle: tan θ = 10 ÷ 3√2 = 2.357…, θ = 67.010…° = 67.0°.
A wedge has a 12 cm by 5 cm rectangular base and a vertical back edge of 4 cm. Find AF and the angle it makes with the base. Higher
Base diagonal: AC = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 cm.
AF: F is directly above C, so AF² = 13² + 4² = 185, AF = √185 = 13.601… = 13.6 cm.
Angle FAC: tan θ = 4 ÷ 13, θ = 17.102…° = 17.1°.
Working backwards: a cuboid has edges 5 cm and 10 cm and a space diagonal of 15 cm. Find the third edge. Higher
d² = a² + b² + c² → 15² = 5² + 10² + c².
225 = 25 + 100 + c² → c² = 100.
c = 10 cm. (Subtract the squares you know, just like finding a shorter side in 2D.)
Same 8 × 6 × 5 cuboid: find the angle between AG and the front face ABFE. Higher
Which plane? The front face contains A, B, F, E. The perpendicular from G to that face lands on F (GF = BC = 6 cm is at right angles to the front face).
Shadow: AF, the diagonal of the front face: AF = √(8² + 5²) = √89 = 9.433… cm.
Triangle AFG (right angle at F): tan θ = GF ÷ AF = 6 ÷ 9.433…, θ = 32.456…° = 32.5°.

Quick check

What you've learned

  • Every 3D length or angle question hides right-angled triangles. Find one, draw it flat, label it, then use Pythagoras or SOH CAH TOA.
  • Space diagonal of a cuboid: base diagonal first (√(a² + b²)), then up: d = √(a² + b² + c²).
  • The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection (shadow) on the plane. Drop a perpendicular from the end of the line to the plane; the shadow is the adjacent side.
  • Pyramids: slant edge uses half the base diagonal; slant height of a face uses half the base width.
  • Wedges: the sloping diagonal is a space diagonal; its angle with the base uses the base diagonal.
  • Keep surds or full calculator values in the middle; round only at the end.
