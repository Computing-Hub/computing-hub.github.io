Fractional and Negative Indices

You know 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2. But what could 2−3 mean? Or 82/3? You can't multiply 8 by itself two-thirds of a time. This page shows that the index laws you already know force these powers to have one sensible meaning: a negative index means "one over", and a fractional index means a root.

DfE GCSE subject content (Number): calculate with roots, and with integer indices; Higher tier: calculate with fractional indices; estimate powers and roots of any given positive number

What you need from KS3

  • Index notation: in an, a is the base and n is the index (or power): how many copies of a are multiplied. 24 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16.
  • The three index laws: am × an = am+n, am ÷ an = am−n, (am)n = amn. Full recap on the index laws page.
  • Roots: √25 = 5 because 5² = 25; ∛8 = 2 because 2³ = 8. See powers and roots.
  • Reciprocals: the reciprocal of a number is 1 divided by it, so you flip a fraction upside down. The reciprocal of 5 is 15; the reciprocal of 23 is 32.

1 · Recap: why the index laws work

The laws aren't rules to memorise; they come from counting factors. Pick a law and watch the copies of a. Every idea on this page comes from insisting that these laws keep working for any index.

2 · Zero and negative indices

Look at the powers of 2 going down: 23 = 8, 22 = 4, 21 = 2. Every time the index drops by 1, the value is divided by 2. There's no reason to stop at 21. Keep the pattern going and see what 20, 2−1, 2−2 must be.

The pattern says two things. Anything to the power 0 is 1 (as long as the base isn't 0). And a negative index means the reciprocal: 2−3 is not negative; it's 12³ = 18.

a0 = 1     a−n = 1an

The division law gives the same answer. a3 ÷ a5 = a3−5 = a−2 by the law. But writing out the factors and cancelling gives 1a². Both must be right, so a−2 = 1a². Change the counts and watch the factors cancel in pairs.

Exam trap: a negative index does not make the answer negative. 5−2 = 125, not −25 and not −10. The minus sign in the index means "flip", not "make negative". Also: 70 = 1, not 0 and not 7.

3 · Fractional indices: a1/n is a root Higher

What number should 91/2 be? Use the multiplication law: 91/2 × 91/2 = 91/2 + 1/2 = 91 = 9. So 91/2 is a number that multiplies by itself to give 9. That's the square root: 91/2 = √9 = 3. In the same way, three copies of 81/3 multiply to 81, so 81/3 = ∛8 = 2, the cube root.

a1/n = ⁿ√a

The picture makes it concrete: a1/2 is the side of a square with area a, and a1/3 is the edge of a cube with volume a. For bases that aren't perfect powers, such as 101/2, the answer is a surd (see the surds page) and your calculator gives a decimal: 101/2 = 3.16 to 3 s.f.

4 · am/n: root first, then power Higher

Now the numerator isn't 1. Split the fraction using the power-of-a-power law: m/n = (1/n) × m, so

am/n = (ⁿ√a)m = ⁿ√(am)

The denominator is the root and the numerator is the power. A memory hook: the root is at the bottom, like the roots of a tree. Both orders give the same answer, but taking the root first keeps the numbers small. Compare the two routes:

Common mistakes with fractional indices

  • Multiplying by the index. 82/3 is not 8 × 23. An index is never a multiplier.
  • Getting the root and power the wrong way round. In 82/3 the 3 (bottom) is the cube root and the 2 (top) is the square. (∛8)² = 4; (√8)³ is something else.
  • Dividing instead of rooting. 161/2 = 4, not 8. Half the index is not half the number.

5 · Negative fractional indices Higher

Put sections 2 and 4 together. In a−m/n each part of the index has one job: the minus sign flips, the denominator roots, the numerator powers. Do them in that order: flip, root, power. Pick an expression and run it through the machine.

6 · Fractions to a negative power

A negative index means "1 over". When the base is already a fraction, 1 over a fraction is the fraction flipped:

(ab)−n = (ba)n

Why: (23)−2 = 1 ÷ (23)² = 1 ÷ 49 = 94, which is (32)². So flip first and the minus sign disappears. With a fractional index (Higher), root the top and bottom separately after flipping.

Expressions marked H have fractional indices: Higher tier.

7 · Solving index equations

To solve an equation like 2x = 18, write both sides as powers of the same base. Then the indices must be equal. Here 8 = 23, so 18 = 2−3, and 2x = 2−3 gives x = −3.

The number line below is in "index space": each tick is one more factor of the common base. The left-hand side jumps along in steps of its base's power; x counts how many jumps it takes to land on the right-hand side. (Higher questions need a fraction of a jump: 4x = 8 means 22x = 23, so 2x = 3 and x = 32.)

Worked examples

Evaluate 5−2
Negative index means reciprocal: 5−2 = 15².
Work out the power: 5² = 25, so 5−2 = 125 (= 0.04).
Not −25 and not −10: the answer is positive and small.
Evaluate 163/4 Higher
Denominator 4 means fourth root: ⁴√16 = 2, because 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 16.
Numerator 3 means cube it: 2³ = 8.
Answer: 163/4 = 8. (Power first would need ⁴√(16³) = ⁴√4096, the same 8 but much harder.)
Evaluate 27−2/3 Higher
Flip (the minus sign): 27−2/3 = 1272/3.
Root (the 3): ∛27 = 3.
Power (the 2): 3² = 9.
Answer: 19.
Evaluate (49)−3/2 Higher
Flip the fraction to remove the minus: (94)3/2.
Square root top and bottom: √9 = 3 and √4 = 2, giving 32.
Cube top and bottom: 3³ = 27 and 2³ = 8.
Answer: 278 (or 338 as a mixed number).
Solve 2x = 132
Write 32 as a power of 2: 32 = 25.
One over it is a negative power: 132 = 2−5.
Compare indices: 2x = 2−5, so x = −5.
Solve 4x = 18 Higher
Common base 2: 4 = 2², so 4x = (2²)x = 22x. And 18 = 2−3.
Compare indices: 2x = −3, so x = −32.
Check: 4−3/2 = 1(√4)³ = 12³ = 18 ✓
Exam-style: write 1x√x in the form xk Higher
Write the root as a power: √x = x1/2, so x√x = x1 × x1/2 = x3/2.
One over means a negative index: 1x3/2 = x−3/2.
Answer: k = −32.

Quick check

What you've learned

  • The index laws come from counting factors, and they decide what every other index must mean.
  • a0 = 1 (for any a ≠ 0), from the halving pattern or from an ÷ an.
  • a−n = 1/an: a negative index means the reciprocal, not a negative answer.
  • a1/n = ⁿ√a, because n copies of a1/n multiply to a1. (Higher)
  • am/n = (ⁿ√a)m: the denominator roots, the numerator powers. Root first to keep the numbers small. (Higher)
  • a−m/n: flip, root, power. (Higher)
  • (a/b)−n = (b/a)n: flip the fraction and the minus sign goes.
  • To solve index equations, write both sides as powers of the same base and set the indices equal.
