Trigonometry and Exact Values

At KS3 you met sin, cos and tan in a single right-angled triangle. At GCSE the triangles get hidden inside bigger pictures: two triangles sharing a side, a boat seen from a cliff, a ladder against a wall. You'll also learn five special angles whose sin, cos and tan you can write down exactly, with no calculator, because they come from two very simple triangles.

GCSE subject content G20: know the trigonometric ratios and apply them to find angles and lengths in right-angled triangles. G21: know the exact values of sin θ and cos θ for θ = 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, and of tan θ for θ = 0°, 30°, 45° and 60°.

1 · Recap: SOH CAH TOA

This page builds on the KS3 page Trigonometric ratios; go there first if the words below are new. Here is the short version. In a right-angled triangle, pick one of the two acute angles (less than 90°) and call it θ ("theta"). The sides are named from θ:

  • hypotenuse (H): the longest side, opposite the right angle. It never changes.
  • opposite (O): the side across the triangle from θ, not touching it.
  • adjacent (A): the side next to θ that is not the hypotenuse.
SOHsin θ = O ÷ H
CAHcos θ = A ÷ H
TOAtan θ = O ÷ A

Why does this work? Every right-angled triangle with the same angle θ is the same shape (they are similar), just bigger or smaller. So the ratio O ÷ H is the same for all of them, and your calculator has those ratios stored. Change the size below and watch the ratios stay fixed; change θ and they change.

sin θ = O ÷ H
cos θ = A ÷ H
tan θ = O ÷ A

2 · Choose, substitute, solve

Every right-angled trig question follows the same four steps:

  1. Label the sides H, O, A from the angle you know (or want).
  2. Choose the ratio that uses the two sides in the question: the one you know and the one you want. Cross out the third side; it plays no part.
  3. Substitute into the ratio, e.g. sin 35° = x ÷ 10.
  4. Solve: if x is on top, multiply (x = 10 × sin 35°); if x is underneath, x and the sin swap places (x = 10 ÷ sin 35°). For an angle, use the inverse: θ = sin⁻¹(O ÷ H) (SHIFT then sin on most calculators).

Practise below. The triangle turns round each time, so you have to label it properly rather than rely on where the sides usually are. Give lengths to 3 s.f. (3 significant figures) and angles to 1 d.p. If you go wrong, the feedback tries to name the mistake.

Calculator trap: degrees mode. Check that your calculator shows D or DEG. In radians mode, sin 30 gives −0.988 instead of 0.5, and every answer is wrong. A quick test: sin 30 should be exactly 0.5.

3 · Multi-step problems

GCSE questions often hide two right-angled triangles in one diagram, sharing a side. You can't reach the answer in one step, so you work out the shared side first and then use it in the second triangle. The trick is to look for the side that belongs to both triangles.

Don't round in the middle. Keep the full calculator value of the shared side (use the ANS button or store it in memory) and only round the final answer. Rounding the shared side first can change the last digit of your final answer, and that can lose the accuracy mark.

4 · Angles of elevation and depression

Two new words, both measured from the horizontal (a flat, level line):

  • The angle of elevation is the angle you turn your eyes up through, from looking straight ahead to looking at something higher, such as the top of a cliff.
  • The angle of depression is the angle you turn your eyes down through, from looking straight ahead to looking at something lower, such as a boat below the cliff.

Drag the boat (or use the slider). The horizontal line at the cliff top and the sea are parallel, so the line of sight makes a Z shape: the angle of depression from the cliff equals the angle of elevation from the boat (alternate angles). That gives a right-angled triangle with the height as the opposite side and the distance along the sea as the adjacent: tan is usually the ratio you need.

Try it: real problems

Exam traps with elevation and depression

  • Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical (the cliff face). It is always from the horizontal. If you are given the angle with the vertical, the angle with the horizontal is 90° minus it.
  • Forgetting the observer's eye height: if the angle is measured from 1.6 m above the ground, add 1.6 m to the height you calculate.
  • Not drawing a diagram. Sketch the horizontal line first, then the line of sight, then mark the right angle.

5 · Exact values from two special triangles

For most angles, sin, cos and tan are endless decimals: sin 35° = 0.573576…. But for 30°, 45° and 60° the answers are simple fractions and square roots (surds), which you can work out exactly from two triangles. GCSE papers expect you to know these and use them on the non-calculator paper. Step through each derivation.

Two of these values are often written another way. 1/√2 and √2/2 are equal: multiply top and bottom of 1/√2 by √2 and you get √2/2. This is called rationalising the denominator (see the Surds page). In the same way 1/√3 = √3/3. Either form earns the marks.

What about 0° and 90°?

Make the hypotenuse exactly 1. Then sin θ = O ÷ 1 = O and cos θ = A ÷ 1 = A, so the opposite side is sin θ and the adjacent side is cos θ. Squash θ down to 0°: the triangle flattens, the opposite side shrinks to 0 and the adjacent side stretches to 1. Push θ up to 90°: the opposite becomes 1 and the adjacent becomes 0.

sin θ (opposite)
cos θ (adjacent)
tan θ = sin ÷ cos

So sin 0° = 0, cos 0° = 1, tan 0° = 0 ÷ 1 = 0; and sin 90° = 1, cos 90° = 0. But tan 90° = 1 ÷ 0, and you can't divide by zero: tan 90° is undefined. (As θ gets close to 90°, tan θ gets enormous; try 89° on a calculator.)

6 · The exact values table

Here is everything in one table. You need to know it, and there's a pattern that makes it easy to rebuild in an exam:

  • sin for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° is √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2, which simplify to 0, ½, √2/2, √3/2, 1.
  • cos is the same list backwards (because cos θ = sin(90° − θ): the two acute angles of a right-angled triangle swap opposite and adjacent).
  • tan = sin ÷ cos, because (O/H) ÷ (A/H) = O/A.

7 · Using exact values without a calculator

On the non-calculator paper, a triangle with a 30°, 45° or 60° angle is a signal: use the exact value. Write the ratio, replace sin, cos or tan with its exact value, and tidy up. Leave surds as surds: 5√3 is exact, 8.66 is not.

tan 60° = x ÷ 5
x = 5 × tan 60° = 5 × √3 = 5√3 cm

Each question below is new. Pick an answer: wrong choices explain the likely mistake.

Worked examples

Multi-step: AB = 15 cm, angle BAD = 40°, angle BCD = 25°, BD is perpendicular to AC. Find BC to 3 s.f.
Spot the shared side. BD belongs to both triangles ABD and CBD. Find it first.
Triangle ABD, angle 40° at A: AB = 15 is the hypotenuse (opposite the right angle at D), BD is opposite 40°. O and H → sin.
sin 40° = BD ÷ 15 → BD = 15 × sin 40° = 9.641814… cm. Keep this on the calculator.
Triangle CBD, angle 25° at C: BD is opposite 25°, BC is the hypotenuse. O and H → sin again.
sin 25° = 9.641814… ÷ BC → BC = 9.641814… ÷ sin 25° = 22.814… cm.
Answer: BC = 22.8 cm (3 s.f.). (BC is opposite the right angle in the right-hand triangle, so it should be longer than BD = 9.64 ✓.)
Angle of depression: from the top of an 80 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is 23°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff?
Diagram: horizontal line from the cliff top, line of sight 23° below it. By alternate angles, the angle of elevation from the boat to the cliff top is also 23°.
Label from the 23° at the boat: the cliff (80 m) is opposite, the distance d is adjacent. O and A → tan.
tan 23° = 80 ÷ d → d = 80 ÷ tan 23° = 188.468… m.
Answer: 188 m (3 s.f.). Trap: 80 × tan 23° = 34.0 m puts d on the top of the fraction by mistake. A shallow angle of 23° means the boat is far away, so d should be much bigger than 80 ✓.
Angle of elevation with eye height: Ana stands 25 m from a tower. Her eyes are 1.6 m above the ground and the angle of elevation of the top is 38°. How tall is the tower?
The right-angled triangle starts at eye level, not at the ground. Its horizontal side is 25 m (adjacent to 38°) and its vertical side h is the height of the tower above her eyes (opposite).
tan 38° = h ÷ 25 → h = 25 × tan 38° = 19.532… m.
Add the eye height: 19.532… + 1.6 = 21.132… m.
Answer: the tower is 21.1 m tall (3 s.f.). Forgetting the 1.6 m gives 19.5 m, a common lost mark.
Non-calculator: a 6 m ladder leans against a wall at 60° to the ground. Find exactly how high up the wall it reaches.
Label from the 60° at the foot: ladder = hypotenuse = 6; height up the wall h = opposite. O and H → sin.
sin 60° = h ÷ 6 → h = 6 × sin 60°.
Exact value: sin 60° = √3/2, so h = 6 × √3/2 = 3√3 m.
Check: √3 ≈ 1.73, so 3√3 ≈ 5.2 m: less than the 6 m ladder ✓.
Non-calculator: in a right-angled triangle, the side adjacent to a 30° angle is 12 cm. Find the exact length x of the opposite side.
O and A → tan: tan 30° = x ÷ 12, so x = 12 × tan 30°.
Exact value: tan 30° = 1/√3 = √3/3.
x = 12 × √3/3 = (12 ÷ 3)√3 = 4√3 cm. (Or 12/√3, then rationalise: 12√3/3 = 4√3.)
Non-calculator: show that sin 60° × tan 30° + cos 60° = 1.
Substitute exact values: sin 60° = √3/2, tan 30° = 1/√3, cos 60° = 1/2.
sin 60° × tan 30° = √3/2 × 1/√3 = √3/(2√3) = 1/2 (the √3 cancels).
1/2 + cos 60° = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 ✓. In a "show that", write every line: the marks are for the working.
Non-calculator: a right-angled triangle has opposite side 5√3 cm and adjacent side 5 cm. Find the angle θ.
O and A → tan: tan θ = 5√3 ÷ 5 = √3.
Which angle has tan equal to √3? From the table, tan 60° = √3.
θ = 60°. Sense check: the opposite is longer than the adjacent, so θ is more than 45° ✓.

Quick check

What you've learned

  • Label H, O, A from the angle, choose the ratio that uses the two sides in the question (SOH CAH TOA), substitute, and solve: multiply if x is on top, divide if x is underneath, inverse (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) for an angle.
  • In multi-step problems, find the side shared by two triangles first, and keep its full value until the end.
  • Angles of elevation (looking up) and depression (looking down) are measured from the horizontal, and are equal by alternate angles.
  • The isosceles right triangle (1, 1, √2) gives sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2 and tan 45° = 1. Half an equilateral triangle (1, √3, 2) gives sin 30° = cos 60° = ½, sin 60° = cos 30° = √3/2, tan 30° = √3/3 and tan 60° = √3.
  • sin 0° = 0, cos 0° = 1, tan 0° = 0; sin 90° = 1, cos 90° = 0, tan 90° is undefined.
  • Pattern: sin goes √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2; cos is the same backwards; tan = sin ÷ cos.
  • Without a calculator, replace sin, cos or tan by its exact value and leave surds in the answer.
