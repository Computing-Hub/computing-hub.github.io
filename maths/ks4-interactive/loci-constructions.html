Loci and Constructions

A goat is tied to a post with a 5 m rope: where can it graze? A phone mast reaches 30 km: which villages get a signal? A locus (plural loci, said "low-sigh") is the set of all points that obey a rule. Four basic loci, drawn with a ruler and compasses, combine to answer questions like these.

GCSE subject content G2: use the standard ruler and compass constructions (perpendicular bisector of a line segment, constructing a perpendicular to a given line from/at a given point, bisecting a given angle); use these to construct given figures and solve loci problems; know that the perpendicular distance from a point to a line is the shortest distance to the line.

1 · Recap: the two key constructions

The KS3 page Constructions shows every construction step by step. Two of them are loci in disguise, so here's a quick reminder. Leave your compass arcs showing: in an exam they are the evidence that you constructed rather than measured.

2 · A fixed distance from a point: a circle

All the points exactly 3 cm from a point P form a circle with centre P and radius 3 cm. Points less than 3 cm away are inside it; that region is the locus "within 3 cm of P". Tap anywhere to test a point.

1 grid square = 1 cm. Drag P; tap elsewhere to drop a test point.

3 · A fixed distance from a line segment: a stadium

For points a fixed distance d from a line segment AB, the shortest distance to the segment is measured at right angles to it (the perpendicular distance), except beyond the ends, where the nearest point of the segment is A or B itself. So the locus is two straight lines parallel to AB, joined by two semicircles centred on A and B: a stadium (or running-track) shape. A common mistake is to draw a rectangle with square corners.

Drag A, B or the test point T.

4 · Equidistant from two points: the perpendicular bisector

Points that are the same distance from A as from B lie on the perpendicular bisector of AB: the line through the midpoint of AB at 90° to it. Everything on A's side of it is closer to A. Drag P and watch the two distances.

6 · Combining loci: regions

Real questions combine several rules. Draw each locus, decide which side of it you want, and shade the region where all the rules are true at once. The rectangular field is 12 m by 8 m. Tick the rules, adjust them, and tap the field to test a point.

Drag A and B. Tap the field to test a point.

Exam tips: use a sharp pencil and keep compass arcs visible; draw "within" boundaries solid and "more than" boundaries dashed if asked; check whether "closer to A" means A's side of the bisector; and always check the scale (e.g. 1 cm = 10 km) before setting your compasses.

Worked examples

Construct the perpendicular bisector of a line AB 8 cm long.
Open the compasses to more than half of AB (say 5 cm).
With the point on A, draw arcs above and below AB. Without changing the compasses, repeat from B so the arcs cross at two points.
Join the two crossing points with a ruler. This line crosses AB at its midpoint (4 cm from each end) at 90°. Every point on it is the same distance from A and from B.
Bisect an angle of 70°.
With the compass point on the vertex, draw an arc crossing both arms.
From each crossing point, draw arcs (same radius) that cross between the arms.
Join the vertex to that crossing point. Each half is 35°; every point on the line is equidistant from the two arms.
Find the area of the region within 2 cm of a line segment 6 cm long. Give your answer in terms of π and to 3 s.f.
The region is a stadium: a 6 cm × 4 cm rectangle (2 cm each side of the segment) plus two semicircles of radius 2 cm at the ends.
Rectangle = 6 × 4 = 24 cm². Two semicircles = one circle = π × 2² = 4π cm².
Area = 24 + 4π cm² = 36.566… = 36.6 cm². (Its perimeter is 6 + 6 + 2π × 2 = 12 + 4π cm.)
A goat is tied to a post in the corner of a rectangular field with a 5 m rope. Find the area it can graze.
The locus "within 5 m of the post" is a circle, but the fences cut it off: only the quarter inside the field (the corner is 90°) is available.
Area = ¼ × π × 5² = 6.25π = 19.634… = 19.6 m².
A goat is tied with a 4 m rope to a ring that slides along a straight 10 m rail fixed to the ground in the middle of a field. Describe and find the grazing area.
The goat can reach any point within 4 m of the rail: a stadium shape.
Rectangle 10 × 8 = 80 m² plus a full circle π × 4² = 16π = 50.265… m².
Total = 80 + 16π = 130.265… = 130 m² (3 s.f.).
On a map with scale 1 cm : 10 km, a mast at M has a range of 30 km. Shade the places within range that are closer to town A than to town B.
30 km ÷ 10 = 3 cm on the map: draw a circle of radius 3 cm centred on M. Inside it is in range.
Construct the perpendicular bisector of AB. A's side is closer to A.
Shade the part of the circle on A's side of the bisector (where both rules are true).
Why is the distance from a point to a line measured at right angles?
Drop a perpendicular from point P to the line, meeting it at F. Any other point G on the line makes a right-angled triangle PFG with PG as the hypotenuse.
The hypotenuse is the longest side, so PG > PF. The perpendicular PF is therefore the shortest distance.

Quick check

What you've learned

  • A locus is the set of points obeying a rule.
  • Fixed distance from a point: a circle. Within that distance: the inside of the circle.
  • Fixed distance from a line segment: a stadium (parallel lines with semicircular ends), not a rectangle.
  • Equidistant from two points: the perpendicular bisector. Equidistant from two lines: the angle bisector.
  • The shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular distance.
  • For combined loci, draw each one, choose the correct side, and shade where all the conditions hold. Keep construction arcs visible and use the scale.
