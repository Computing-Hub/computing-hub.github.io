Arcs, Sectors and Segments

A slice of pizza is a sector; its crust is an arc. Both are simply a fraction of the whole circle, and the fraction is the angle out of 360°. Once you see that, every formula on this page writes itself, including how to work backwards to the angle or radius, and (at Higher) how to find the area of a segment, the piece left when you cut a straight line across a circle.

GCSE subject content G17: calculate perimeters of 2D shapes, including circles, and areas of circles and composite shapes. G18: calculate arc lengths, angles and areas of sectors of circles. G23 (Higher): Area = ½ab sin C, used here for the area of a segment.

1 · Recap: the whole circle

From the KS3 page Circles: the distance round a circle of radius r is the circumference C = 2πr (or πd), and the space inside is the area A = πr². Leaving π as a symbol gives an exact answer "in terms of π", like 10π; pressing the π button gives a rounded decimal.

Circumference 2πr
Area πr²

2 · Arc length: a fraction of the circumference

An arc is part of the circumference. A sector is the slice between two radii, and its angle at the centre is θ. A full turn is 360°, so a sector with angle θ is the fraction θ/360 of the circle. A 90° sector is ¼ of the circle, so its arc is ¼ of the circumference. In general:

Arc length = θ/360 × 2πr

The bar is the whole circumference unrolled; the orange part is the arc.

3 · Sector area: a fraction of the area

The same fraction applies to area. A 60° sector is 60/360 = ⅙ of the circle, so its area is ⅙ of πr². Six of them fit together to make the whole circle.

Sector area = θ/360 × πr²

Common mistakes: using the diameter in πr²; mixing up the two formulas (arc is a length, from 2πr; sector is an area, from πr²); and using θ/180 or θ/100 instead of θ/360.

4 · Perimeter of a sector

The perimeter is the whole way round the outside of the slice: along one radius, round the arc, and back along the other radius. Forgetting the two straight radii is the most common lost mark. Walk round the edge:

Perimeter of a sector = arc length + 2r

5 · Answers in terms of π

"Give your answer in terms of π" means: don't press the π button. Work out the number that multiplies π, simplify any fraction, and leave π as a letter. The trick is to simplify θ/360 first, then multiply by r² (for area) or 2r (for arc).

A perimeter in terms of π has two parts that can't be combined: for example 4π/3 + 12 cm. (You can't add π's and plain numbers, just like you can't simplify x + 12.)

6 · Working backwards and practice

If you know the arc length or the area, write the formula, substitute what you know, and solve for the unknown. For the angle: find what fraction of the circle you have, then multiply by 360. For the radius: rearrange; if it came from r², take a square root at the end.

θ = arc ÷ (2πr) × 360
θ = area ÷ (πr²) × 360
r = arc × 360 ÷ (2πθ)
r = √(area × 360 ÷ (πθ))

7 · Area of a segment Higher

A segment is the region between a chord and an arc. A chord AB cuts the sector OAB into two pieces: the triangle OAB and the segment. So

Segment = sector − triangle = θ/360 × πr² − ½r² sin θ

The triangle has two sides equal to r with the angle θ between them, so its area is ½ × r × r × sin θ (from area = ½ab sin C; see Sine and cosine rules). Slide θ past 180°: the major segment is the sector plus a triangle, and the formula still works because sin θ is negative then.

Worked examples

Find the arc length of a sector with angle 72° and radius 10 cm, to 1 d.p.
Fraction: 72/360 = 1/5.
Arc = 1/5 × 2 × π × 10 = 1/5 × 20π = 4π = 12.566… cm.
Answer: 12.6 cm (1 d.p.), or exactly 4π cm.
Find the area of a sector with angle 135° and radius 8 cm, in terms of π.
Fraction: 135/360 = 3/8 (divide top and bottom by 45).
Area = 3/8 × π × 8² = 3/8 × 64π = 24π.
Answer: 24π cm² (= 75.4 cm² to 1 d.p.).
Find the perimeter of a sector with angle 50° and radius 9 cm, to 1 d.p.
Arc = 50/360 × 2 × π × 9 = 5/36 × 18π = 2.5π = 7.853… cm.
Add both radii: perimeter = 7.853… + 9 + 9 = 25.853… cm.
Answer: 25.9 cm. (Just the arc, 7.9 cm, is the classic wrong answer.)
A sector has angle 40° and radius 6 cm. Find its area and perimeter in terms of π.
Fraction: 40/360 = 1/9.
Area = 1/9 × 36π = 4π cm².
Arc = 1/9 × 12π = 12π/9 = 4π/3. Perimeter = 4π/3 + 12 cm. (Leave it as two terms.)
Working backwards: a sector of radius 10 cm has area 60 cm². Find its angle to 1 d.p.
Formula: θ/360 × π × 10² = 60, so θ/360 × 100π = 60.
Fraction of the circle: θ/360 = 60 ÷ 100π = 0.19098…
θ = 0.19098… × 360 = 68.754… → 68.8°.
Working backwards: a sector with angle 80° has area 50 cm². Find the radius to 3 s.f.
80/360 × πr² = 50.
r² = 50 × 360 ÷ (80π) = 71.619…
r = √71.619… = 8.4628… → 8.46 cm. (71.6 is r²: don't forget the square root.)
Segment: a chord cuts off a segment from a circle of radius 10 cm, with the chord subtending 70° at the centre. Find the area of the minor segment to 3 s.f. Higher
Sector = 70/360 × π × 10² = 61.086… cm².
Triangle = ½ × 10 × 10 × sin 70° = 50 × 0.93969… = 46.984… cm².
Segment = 61.086… − 46.984… = 14.101… → 14.1 cm².
Trap: using ½ × 10 × 10 = 50 for the triangle (forgetting sin 70°) gives 11.1 cm²: that's the area of a right-angled triangle, and this one isn't right-angled.

Quick check

What you've learned

  • A sector with angle θ is the fraction θ/360 of the whole circle.
  • Arc length = θ/360 × 2πr (a length). Sector area = θ/360 × πr² (an area).
  • Perimeter of a sector = arc + 2r: don't forget the two radii.
  • In terms of π: simplify θ/360 first, multiply by 2r or r², and leave π as a symbol.
  • Working backwards: find the fraction of the circle, × 360 for the angle; rearrange for r, with a square root if it came from r².
  • Higher: segment = sector − triangle = θ/360 × πr² − ½r² sin θ.
