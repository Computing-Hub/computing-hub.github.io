Transformations

Slide it, flip it, turn it, resize it. Four moves cover every exam question — and each one has a short list of details you must give when you "describe fully".

National curriculum KS3: identify and construct congruent and similar shapes, including by translation, rotation, reflection and enlargement

1 · One shape, four moves

A transformation moves a shape without changing what it is. The original is the object (teal); the moved copy is the image (purple). Try each move on the triangle.

2 · Describing a transformation

Exam questions say "describe fully". Each move has its own required details — miss one and the mark is gone.

MoveYou must giveExample answer
Translationthe vectortranslation by (3 over −2)
Reflectionthe mirror linereflection in the line x = 1
Rotationangle, direction, centrerotation 90° anticlockwise about (0, 0)
Enlargementscale factor, centreenlargement scale factor 2, centre (0, 0)

Which moves keep the shape the same size? Translation, reflection and rotation — the image is congruent to the object. Enlargement changes the size but keeps the proportions — the image is similar.

Worked examples

Translate triangle A by the vector (4 over −3).
Top number: 4 right. Bottom number: 3 down. Move every corner the same way.
(1, 2) → (5, −1)  (3, 2) → (7, −1)  (1, 5) → (5, 2)
Reflect the point (3, 2) in the y-axis, then in the x-axis.
y-axis flips the x-coordinate; x-axis flips the y-coordinate.
(3, 2) → (−3, 2) → (−3, −2)
Enlarge a 3 cm × 2 cm rectangle, scale factor 2. What happens to the area?
Lengths double, so the area is multiplied by 2² = 4, not 2.
6 cm × 4 cm = 24 cm² = 4 × the original 6 cm²

What you've learned

Translation slides by a vector. Reflection flips in a mirror line. Rotation turns by an angle and direction about a centre. Enlargement multiplies lengths by a scale factor from a centre. The first three keep the shape congruent; enlargement makes it similar. To describe fully: vector; mirror line; angle + direction + centre; scale factor + centre.
