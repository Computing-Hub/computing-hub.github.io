Order of Operations

One sum, one answer. BIDMAS is the agreed order every calculator and every examiner uses: Brackets, Indices, Divide and Multiply, Add and Subtract. The tie-break rule — equal ranks go left to right — is where the marks hide.

National curriculum KS3: use conventional notation for the priority of operations, including brackets, powers and roots

1 · Why order matters

What is 3 + 4 × 2? If you read left to right you get 14. A calculator says 11. Maths needs one agreed order, or every sum would have many answers.

Left to right (wrong)
3 + 4 × 2
= 7 × 2
= 14 ✗
Multiply first (right)
3 + 4 × 2
= 3 + 8
= 11 ✓

2 · The order: BIDMAS

Work down this list. Each level beats the ones below it.

OrderLettersMeans
1BBrackets first, always
2IIndices (powers and roots): 3² before anything else outside brackets
3D MDivide and Multiply — equal rank, do them left to right
4A SAdd and Subtract — equal rank, do them left to right

The common trap: D does not beat M, and A does not beat S. They are ties, settled left to right. 10 − 4 + 2 is 6 + 2 = 8, not 10 − 6 = 4.

4 · Brackets change everything

Brackets say "me first", whatever is inside. The same numbers can give very different answers.

No brackets
3 + 4 × 2 = 3 + 8 = 11
20 − 6 ÷ 2 = 20 − 3 = 17
With brackets
(3 + 4) × 2 = 7 × 2 = 14
(20 − 6) ÷ 2 = 14 ÷ 2 = 7

Worked examples

Work out 5 + 3 × 2²
No brackets. Indices first: 2² = 4. Then multiply: 3 × 4 = 12. Then add.
5 + 3 × 2² = 5 + 3 × 4 = 5 + 12 = 17
Work out (10 − 4)² ÷ 9
Brackets first: 10 − 4 = 6. Then the index: 6² = 36. Then divide.
(10 − 4)² ÷ 9 = 6² ÷ 9 = 36 ÷ 9 = 4
Work out 24 ÷ 6 × 2
÷ and × are a tie, so go left to right. (Doing 6 × 2 first gives 2 — the trap.)
24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8

Quick check

What you've learned

Brackets first. Indices next. Divide and multiply are a tie, done left to right; so are add and subtract. Writing one step per line keeps the order honest and makes mistakes easy to spot. If you want a different order, you must add brackets — that is what they are for.
