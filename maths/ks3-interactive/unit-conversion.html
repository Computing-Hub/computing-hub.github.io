Units and Compound Units

Every measurement is a number and a unit, and the unit is half the information. Converting between units is multiplying or dividing by the right factor; compound units like km/h and g/cm³ are two measurements divided, and they convert in two steps.

National curriculum KS3: change freely between related standard units (time, length, area, volume/capacity, mass) and compound units (speed, rates of pay, prices, density) in numerical contexts

1 · The metric ladder

Metric units step by powers of ten: 10 mm in a cm, 100 cm in a m, 1000 m in a km. Converting is moving along the ladder. Going to a smaller unit you need more of them, so multiply; going to a bigger unit, divide. Pick a value and two units; the ladder lights up the path.

Time is the odd one out. 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day. So 2.5 hours is 2 h 30 min, not 2 h 50 min, and 1 h 45 min is 1.75 h. Convert minutes to a decimal by dividing by 60 before using them in a speed calculation.

2 · Area and volume units: the trap

1 m = 100 cm, so surely 1 m² = 100 cm²? No. A square metre is 100 cm by 100 cm, which is 10 000 cm². And a cubic metre is 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm³. The conversion factor gets squared for area and cubed for volume. The picture makes it hard to forget.

3 · Speed: a compound unit

Speed is distance divided by time, so its unit is a distance unit per a time unit: m/s, km/h, mph. "Per" means "divided by". The three quantities sit in a triangle: cover the one you want and what's left tells you the calculation.

Converting a speed: two steps

To change m/s into km/h you have to convert both units: metres to kilometres (÷ 1000) and seconds to hours (there are 3600 s in an hour, so × 3600). The combined effect is × 3.6. Going the other way, ÷ 3.6. Watch each step happen separately so the 3.6 isn't magic.

4 · Density: another compound unit

Density is mass divided by volume: how much stuff is packed into each cubic centimetre. Water is 1 g/cm³; iron about 7.9; cork about 0.24. Same triangle, different letters. Below, each cube is 1 cm³; the mass is shared out equally among the cubes, and the density is what one cube weighs.

Worked examples

Convert 2.4 km to cm
km → m: × 1000 → 2400 m.
m → cm: × 100 → 240 000 cm. Smaller unit, bigger number: sensible.
A car travels 180 km in 2 h 15 min. Find its average speed in km/h.
Time as a decimal: 15 min = 15/60 = 0.25 h, so 2.25 h.
Speed = distance ÷ time: 180 ÷ 2.25 = 80 km/h. (Using 2.15 would give 83.7, the classic slip.)
A block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Find its density and decide if it floats.
Density = mass ÷ volume: 540 ÷ 200 = 2.7 g/cm³.
Compare with water (1 g/cm³): denser, so it sinks. (It's aluminium.)
Exam-style: convert 54 km/h to m/s
km → m: 54 km = 54 000 m. So 54 000 m per hour.
per hour → per second: ÷ 3600 → 54 000 ÷ 3600 = 15 m/s.
Shortcut check: 54 ÷ 3.6 = 15 ✓. A sprinter does about 10 m/s, so 15 m/s is a fast cyclist or a slow car. Sensible.

What you've learned

Metric conversions move along a ladder of powers of ten: multiply towards smaller units, divide towards larger. Area factors are the length factor squared and volume factors cubed, which is why 1 m² is 10 000 cm². Compound units are one quantity per another; speed, density, pay and price all sit in the same triangle and all convert in two steps, one for each unit. Time needs care because it isn't decimal.
