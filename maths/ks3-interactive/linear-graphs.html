Linear Graphs

A rule like y = 2x + 1 turns every x into a y. Plot the pairs and they line up, literally, into a straight line. Two numbers describe the whole line: how steep it is (m, the gradient) and where it crosses the y-axis (c, the intercept).

National curriculum KS3: plot graphs of linear functions using a table of values; recognise, sketch and interpret graphs of linear functions; understand gradient and y-intercept in y = mx + c

1 · From a rule to a line

Start with the rule y = 2x + 1. Pick some x-values, substitute each one (see the substitution page), and you get a table of values. Each column is a coordinate pair. Plot them. Step through and watch the line appear.

Three things to notice. The points are evenly spaced: every time x goes up by 1, y goes up by the same amount. That constant step is what makes the graph straight, and it's the number in front of x. And the point where x = 0 is where the line crosses the y-axis.

2 · Gradient: how steep

The gradient (la pendiente) measures steepness as rise over run: how far up for each step across. Draw a right-angled triangle anywhere on the line. Gradient = rise ÷ run. Because the line is straight, every triangle gives the same answer.

Rise ÷ run
Meaning

Sign of the gradient. Positive m: the line goes uphill left to right. Negative m: downhill. m = 0: flat, a horizontal line like y = 3. The bigger the size of m, the steeper. A gradient of 1 is 45°; a gradient of ½ is gentler; a gradient of 3 is steep. Measure rise and run in grid units, not centimetres.

3 · Intercept: where it starts

The y-intercept (la ordenada en el origen) is where the line crosses the y-axis. On the y-axis, x = 0, so put x = 0 into y = mx + c and the mx term vanishes: y = c. The intercept is simply the number on the end. The line "starts" at (0, c) and climbs from there at gradient m.

4 · Sketching without a table

Once you trust m and c, you can draw y = mx + c in two moves with no table at all. Mark (0, c). From there go 1 across and m up (or down if m is negative) to get a second point. Join and extend. Try it: the page gives you an equation, you place the two points.

5 · Reading the equation off a graph

The reverse skill, which exams love: a line is drawn, find its equation. Read c where it crosses the y-axis. Pick two clear grid points on the line, draw the triangle, work out rise ÷ run for m. Write y = mx + c.

7 · Where straight lines show up

Taxi fares and phone bills

£3 to start plus £2 per mile: cost = 2m + 3. The £3 is the intercept (cost before you move), the £2 is the gradient (cost per mile). Every "fixed charge plus rate" is y = mx + c.

Science: conversion graphs

°F = 1.8 °C + 32. Gradient 1.8, intercept 32. Hooke's law, Ohm's law and distance–time at constant speed are all straight lines through the origin: c = 0.

Distance–time graphs

Gradient = speed. A steep line is fast, flat is stopped, downhill is coming back. Reading gradients off a graph is reading speeds.

Computing

y = m*x + c is a one-line function. Drawing a line on screen means computing y for each pixel column x. Linear interpolation in animation and audio is this formula.

Statistics

A line of best fit on a scatter graph is y = mx + c chosen to pass as close as possible to all the points. Its gradient says how much y changes per unit of x.

Later maths

Simultaneous equations are two lines and their crossing point. Differentiation (the first page of this series) finds the gradient of a curve by borrowing the straight-line idea of rise over run.

Worked examples

Draw y = 3x − 2 for x from −2 to 2
Table: x = −2 → −8; −1 → −5; 0 → −2; 1 → 1; 2 → 4.
Plot (−2, −8), (−1, −5), (0, −2), (1, 1), (2, 4). They're in a line; join with a ruler and extend.
Check: crosses the y-axis at −2 (that's c) and rises 3 for each 1 across (that's m) ✓
Find the gradient of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 11)
Rise: 11 − 2 = 9. Run: 4 − 1 = 3.
Gradient: 9 ÷ 3 = 3. (Subtract in the same order top and bottom.)
Write the equation of the line with gradient −2 through (0, 5)
m = −2 is given. c = 5 because (0, 5) is on the y-axis.
y = −2x + 5. Check with another point: x = 1 gives y = 3, and from (0, 5) going 1 across and 2 down does land on (1, 3) ✓
Exam-style: a line has gradient 3 and passes through (2, 1). Find its equation.
Start with what's known: y = 3x + c.
The point must satisfy the equation. Substitute x = 2, y = 1: 1 = 3(2) + c → 1 = 6 + c → c = −5.
Answer: y = 3x − 5. This is the method for any "gradient and a point" question.

Quick check

What you've learned

A linear rule y = mx + c produces points that lie on a straight line. m is the gradient, rise over run, the amount y changes for each step of 1 in x: positive uphill, negative downhill, zero flat. c is the y-intercept, the value of y when x = 0, where the line crosses the y-axis. You can plot from a table of values, or sketch in two moves from (0, c) and the gradient; you can read an equation off a drawn line by finding those same two numbers. Parallel lines share m. Always rearrange to y = … before reading off.
